## G-series: Operations manual verification (added July 11, 2026, per Palmer's directive; author of the manuals: Dennis Lanni)

**G1. The Mitigation Fee Act spans Government Code sections 66000 to 66025.** Source: California Department of Housing and Community Development, "Fees and Exactions" (hcd.ca.gov/housing-element/building-blocks/constraints-fees): "the Mitigation Fee Act (Section 66000-66025)". Corroborated by City of Oakland, City of San Leandro, and City of Clayton fee pages using the identical range. VERIFIED. (Supports INS-202 section 2.2.)

**G2. Government Code 8855 reporting duties for note issuance.** Source: California Debt Financing Guide sections 7.1.1 and 7.1.3 (debtguide-api.treasurer.ca.gov) and CDIAC reporting page (treasurer.ca.gov/cdiac/reporting.asp): Report of Proposed Debt Issuance due 30 days before sale (8855(i)(1)); Report of Final Sale within 21 days (8855(j)); Annual Debt Transparency Report due within seven months of the July 1 to June 30 reporting period, effectively January 31 (8855(k)); and, for Mello-Roos debt specifically, the Yearly Fiscal Status Report due each October 30. VERIFIED. (Supports INS-401 section 4.2 and resolves INS-010's chapter 7 confirm-before-first-levy note.)

**G3. The July 1 to June 30 fiscal and reporting calendar.** Source: the same CDIAC materials (statutory reporting period) and county tax roll cycle documents. VERIFIED. (Supports INS-010 chapter 7.)

**G4. County direct-charge submission mechanics and calendar.** Source: Sacramento County Department of Finance, "Direct Levy Instructions" (finance.saccounty.gov, FY 2025-26 edition): agencies file the county's signature-of-agreement and data submission forms by a stated deadline (July 28, 2025 for that roll year); the county FAQ confirms the Auditor-Controller places roughly 1.8 million direct charges from more than 220 districts as agent and cites Government Code 53356.2 procedures for delinquency removal. VERIFIED. (Supports INS-010 chapter 7 and INS-501 throughout; each county's own instruction letter still governs, exactly as the manuals instruct.)

**G5. School facilities fee statutes: Education Code 17620 authorizes school districts to levy developer fees; Government Code 65995 establishes the fee schedule and limits.** Source: California Debt Financing Guide, Appendix B.1.4.2 Developer Fees (debtguide-api.treasurer.ca.gov): districts "are authorized to impose fees on new development pursuant to Education Code Section 17620" while "Government Code Section 65995 actually establishes the corresponding fee schedule." Corroborated by multiple district developer-fee pages (Temecula Valley, East Side Union, Carlsbad). VERIFIED July 11, 2026. (Supports INS-202's school-facilities-fee roadmap line; found during the package-wide School audit after the citation escaped the earlier statute scan, whose number pattern did not match a five-digit 6xxxx or an Education Code section.)

**G6. Fee-schedule posting mandate: Government Code 65940.1.** Added by AB 1483 (Stats. 2019, Ch. 662), effective January 1, 2020; strengthened by AB 1820 (2024). Every city, county, and special district with a website shall post its current schedule of fees, exactions, and affordability requirements, presented so the fees applying to each parcel are clear, as a written schedule or direct link; update within 30 days of any change; and archive impact-fee nexus studies conducted from January 1, 2018 forward. Sources: 2019 code text at law.justia.com (Section 65940.1), current text at codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-65940-1, AB 1820 amended text at legiscan.com/CA/text/AB1820/id/2971554. VERIFIED July 12, 2026. (This is the legal foundation of the statewide fee database, 02_PROGRAM/DATA, and the fee estimator's instruction to pull the jurisdiction's posted schedule.)

**G7. A CFD may finance another agency's facilities only through a joint community facilities agreement.** Government Code 53316.2(a): a community facilities district may finance facilities owned or operated by a public agency other than the one that created the district "only pursuant to a joint community facilities agreement or a joint exercise of powers agreement." Corroborated: California Debt Financing Guide 3.3.7.2 (multiple-agency financing requires a JCFA or JPA; schools listed among financeable facilities); working examples include Riverside Unified CFD No. 42 (a school-district CFD financing city fees through a JCFA, the mirror image of our case) and the Poway Unified and San Diego JCFA. Sources: codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-53316-2.html; debtguide-api.treasurer.ca.gov (3.3.7.2); riversideca.legistar.com CFD No. 42 agreement. VERIFIED July 12, 2026. (This is the mechanism if question 9 lands on including school fees: one master JCFA per city and district pair, negotiated once, covering future annexations.)

**G8. The ten percent delinquency protection: Government Code 53321(d).** The special tax levied against any parcel for which an occupancy permit for private residential use has been issued may not be increased as a consequence of delinquency or default by the owner of any other parcel within the district by more than 10 percent above the amount that would have been levied had there never been such delinquencies. Sources: City of Roseville, Villages at Sierra Vista CFD No. 1 Official Statement, 2023 Series (roseville.ca.gov, quoting the section); City of Chula Vista CFD No. 17-I formation resolution (pub-chulavista.escribemeetings.com, same language with the occupancy-permit trigger); legislative text at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (AB 373 restating the requirement). VERIFIED July 13, 2026.

**G9. Prepayment authority: Government Code 53344.** Property owners may prepay and permanently satisfy the special tax obligation by cash settlement as permitted under Section 53344, with the conditions and methodology fixed by the district's formation documents and rate-and-method; upon full prepayment a Notice of Cancellation of Special Tax Lien is recorded. Sources: City of Chula Vista CFD No. 17-I rate-and-method ("as permitted under Government Code Section 53344"); legalclarity.org Mello-Roos explainer citing Sections 53321 and 53344 for prepayment and lien cancellation. VERIFIED July 13, 2026. (The program's "defined prepayment right" is therefore a rate-and-method design commitment resting on this statutory authority.)

**G10. Future annexation areas and annexation protections: Government Code 53339.3.** The resolution of intention may identify territory proposed for annexation in the future, with parcels annexed only with the unanimous approval of the owner or owners at the time of annexation (subdivision (b)); the maximum tax rate in the existing district may not be increased as a result of annexation proceedings (subdivision (e)); annexed territory may be taxed for new or additional facilities serving it, and hearings run 30 to 60 days from the resolution (subdivisions (d) and (f)). Sources: full statutory text at law.justia.com (Government Code 53339 through 53339.9); City of Roseville Resolution 18-346 forming The Ranch at Sierra Vista CFD No. 1 with a Future Annexation Area and unanimous-approval annexations (roseville.novusagenda.com). VERIFIED July 13, 2026.
