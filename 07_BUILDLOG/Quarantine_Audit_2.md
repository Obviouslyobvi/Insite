# Quarantine Audit No. 2: The Post-Adoption State

**A fresh, full re-audit of the quarantine folder after the operations manuals were adopted: inventory, integrity, isolation, and verdict.**

Prepared for Dennis Lanni | HGF Management Company | July 11, 2026

---

## Why a second audit

The first quarantine audit judged the content of five mystery manuals and ended with their authorship resolved and their corrected versions adopted into the program library. This second audit, run at Palmer's direction, asks a different question: is the quarantine folder itself now in exactly the state it claims to be in? Four tests, all mechanical, all reproducible: what is in it, has anything been altered, has anything new arrived, and does any of it leak into the live package.

## Inventory: 31 files, every one accounted for

The folder holds 14 original documents (the early whitepaper and developer-checklist drafts, and the five operations manuals, each as the Word file and PDF exactly as received), 16 page-render images created during the original review as visual-inspection rasters (ten pages of the operations manual, six of the whitepaper draft), and the folder's own README. Nothing else.

## Integrity: 30 of 30 content files bit-identical to their arrival

Every content file was hashed today and compared against the exact bytes recorded when it entered the repository (commit ed5bd96, the package's first commit). All 30 match to the bit, and each has exactly one commit in its entire history: its introduction. Nothing has touched Dennis's originals since the moment they were preserved. The one file with a second commit is the README, whose only change is the documented July 11 resolution note recording the adoption, which is precisely the change the record says was made and the only one.

## New arrivals: none

The original mystery was documents appearing unannounced at five in the morning. Today's check: zero untracked files anywhere in the quarantine tree, and the file count matches the documented inventory exactly. Nothing has appeared since.

## Isolation: nothing quarantined reaches the deliverables

The delivered outputs tree contains no quarantined content; the only paths matching the word are the audit documents themselves, which live in the build log where they belong. Nine live documents mention the quarantine, and every one is a record-keeping surface: the build checklist, the gap list, the document library register, the build log, both audits, the verification report, the kill memo, and the root README, plus one sentence in the internal pre-build brief recording the decision to build the whitepaper fresh rather than salvage the quarantined draft. Zero audience-facing surfaces (primer, whitepaper, manuals, marketing, website, deck) reference the quarantine at all.

## Content status, recapped

The five manual originals are superseded by their corrected, adopted versions in the program library and remain here as the authorship record. The two early drafts are superseded by the fresh builds that replaced them. The page rasters are review artifacts from the first audit. Nothing in the folder is pending anything.

## Verdict

The quarantine is a sealed provenance archive in exactly the intended state: complete, unaltered, isolated, and quiet. Zero open items. Recommended disposition: permanent retention as the record of where the operations manuals came from and how they were handled.
