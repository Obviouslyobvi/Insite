## First, the explanation

The document package is complete and verified. Every page that could be built without a decision, a licensed professional, or real-world proof has been built. What remains falls into four buckets, and knowing which bucket an item sits in tells you who has to move: **decisions only Dennis can make**, **signatures only outside professionals can supply**, **proof only the real world can give**, and **small housekeeping the build flagged for human hands**. Nothing below is a surprise; each item traces to the question batch, the sourcing plan, or the verification record. Each entry says what is missing, why the program needs it, and what stays stuck until it exists.

## Bucket 1: Decisions only Dennis can make

**Missing: a legally cleared program name.** A software company already holds a live federal trademark on "INSITE." A trademark attorney's clearance letter (or a backup name) is what stands between the brand and a cease-and-desist after launch. Until it exists, nothing carrying the name should go public: no website, no printed handouts, no filings. Cost of getting it now is small; cost of getting it later is a rebrand. Since this was written the record sharpened: the conflicting registration's full details are confirmed in the evidence register (Reg. 6610415, a live Class 42 software mark held by Insite Software Solutions, now in the Optimizely family), and a ranked bench of 42 fallback names sits in the brand folder with the matching .com and .org verified unregistered for every one of them, so if the attorney says rename, it costs Dennis a decision instead of a scramble.

**Missing: a purchased web address.** Three good ones sit available today: insiteprogram.com, insitecalifornia.com, insitecfd.com. Available means anyone else can take them tomorrow. Until one is bought, there is no public home for the site that is already built, no program email addresses, and no contact line to print on the one-pagers. The naming sprint widened this picture: all three of those remain in the site family that inherits the trademark question, while every one of the 42 fallback names had both its .com and its .org unregistered on July 11, so whichever way the name decision goes, the winning address and its .org twin should be bought the same day.

**Missing: the final word on "School" in the name.** Every document currently reads "Development Impact Fee Financing Program" because school fees are off the menu at launch. If Dennis wants "School" back, it is one find-and-replace, but the decision has to land before anything prints, because a name promising something the program cannot deliver invites the wrong first conversation.

**Missing: the rule for talking about the pilot.** All materials describe it generically ("a small finished-lot subdivision in the Sacramento area"). Specifics (the city, the lot count, dates) need Dennis's sign-off and possibly the landowner's, because it is their project being pointed at. Until then, the deck and whitepaper stay generic, which costs a little persuasive power with capital audiences who love a named example.

**Missing: the final company entity.** HGF Management Company is on every page. If a new legal entity is coming, contracts, bank accounts, and the print run all wait on it. Cheap to change today, expensive after documents are signed.

**Missing: a minimum deal size policy.** The intake form says "determined during feasibility screening" because no honest number exists yet. Nothing is blocked today, but before the program advertises eligibility, Dennis either publishes a floor or deliberately publishes none. The build's recommendation: none until the pilot proves the economics.

**Resolved, July 11: the analysis spreadsheet.** Palmer gave the go-ahead and both formula errors are fixed in a corrected copy (v1.1_corrected), with the original untouched. The fix was proven the honest way: the same test project was fed to both files, and the original dropped construction costs entirely while the corrected copy spreads them month by month exactly as designed. Use the corrected copy for every analysis from now on.

**Resolved, July 11: the quarantined drafts.** Authorship was identified (Dennis Lanni wrote the five operations manuals) and Palmer directed the fix: retired name removed, the builder reference removed, the standard disclaimer added, and every factual claim verified into the register. The corrected manuals now sit in the program library; the originals stay archived as received.

**Missing: confirmation of thirteen draft policy numbers.** Dennis's manuals set thirteen service levels, thresholds, and targets (screening turnaround, payoff-quote speed, a credit-score gate, cycle-time and accuracy targets, the launch org design). Since he wrote them, the ask is light: confirm they still reflect his thinking, or update them. The itemized list is in the Quarantine Audit, sized for the same sitting as the ten questions.

**Missing: the county timing call.** Whether to open the Sacramento County conversation now or after the city pilot closes. Nothing is blocked; this is purely calendar strategy. The build's read: one informal coffee now, the formal ask after the pilot.

## Bucket 2: Signatures only outside professionals can supply

**Missing: a bond attorney.** Every note needs a formal legal opinion or no bank will buy it, and the whitepaper needs this attorney's read before it goes to anyone outside. This is the single most important hire on the list; nothing closes without it.

**Missing: a special tax consultant.** This is the firm that drafts the reusable tax formula template, the document that makes each small deal cheap instead of bespoke. The template is the cost-control heart of the whole model, and it does not exist until this firm is engaged. Selection criteria are already written in the sourcing plan.

**Missing: a tax roll administrator under contract.** The firm that puts each year's special tax on the county bill and tracks who paid. This is also a credibility purchase: cities like San Diego and Vacaville use these same firms, and a young program renting a familiar name looks standard, not weak. The contract terms to demand (data export rights, transition help) are already drafted.

**Missing: the named advisor, on paper.** The retired county district administrator has been a verbal resource. The credibility plan needs the named role confirmed and a one-page bio, because "our advisor ran these districts for the county" only works with a name attached.

## Bucket 3: Proof only the real world can supply

**Missing: a willing city.** The program legally exists only when a city adopts its policies and forms the master financing district, a six-plus-month process. Everything built so far is the pitch for this meeting; the meeting itself has not happened.

**Missing: a signed capital term sheet.** Every interest rate in the package is labeled a target because no purchaser has committed. This is the program's biggest unknown, said out loud in the risk documents: small-note appetite is exactly what the pilot must prove.

**Missing: a closed pilot.** The pilot converts founder estimates into facts: what a deal actually costs, how long it actually takes, and whether the bank actually wires. Until it closes, the honest word for the program is "proposed."

**Missing: one completed annual levy cycle.** The recurring-revenue half of the business (calculate the tax, file with the county, reconcile collections, report to the investor) is designed but unproven. The first cycle after the pilot proves the machine.

**Missing: the consolidated brand manual.** A one-page Brand Kit and real logo masters exist; the legacy brand-manual draft should be merged into a single v1.0, and that waits on the name decision above.

**Missing: contact details on every material.** Deliberately blank ("details added at launch") because the address, phone, and email depend on the entity and domain decisions. Five minutes of edits once those land.

**Missing: whitepaper version 1.1.** Version 1.0 is done and verified, but the releasable version needs the bond attorney's review, the consultant names filled in, and the pilot-description decision. It is the same document with the blanks answered.

**Resolved, July 11: the operations manual series.** Adopted with corrections instead of rebuilt, once the author turned out to be Dennis. Five verified manuals now cover program operations, intake, execution, annual administration, and agency onboarding. The only open thread is his confirmation of the thirteen draft numbers above.

Answer the ten questions and confirm the thirteen draft policy numbers (still a single sitting), engage the bond attorney and special tax consultant (they gate everything legal), book the city briefing (the six-month clock starts only when they say yes), and let the pilot answer the rest.
