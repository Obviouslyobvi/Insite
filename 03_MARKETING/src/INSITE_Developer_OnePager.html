FOR RESIDENTIAL DEVELOPERS  ·  2 TO 50 LOT PROJECTS

The fees are due before the first home sells.
Most of that cash doesn't have to be yours.

You're at the permit counter with a finished map and a six-figure fee bill, months before closing day. Today that means writing the check from working capital or borrowing private money at 9.5 to 15 percent. INSITE exists to give small projects the same tool large master-planned communities have used for decades: finance the fees through a voluntary special tax on your own lots, collected with the property tax bill.

The problem, in numbers

$23,455 average California impact fee per single-family home (2015), nearly 3x the national average
Total development charges can exceed $150,000 per unit in some cities
Private money runs 9.5 to 15 percent, due long before homes close

What financing looks like

A note sized to your project alone, repaid over decades through an annual special tax on the lots. The obligation belongs to the land, transfers automatically at sale, is disclosed to buyers, and any owner can pay it off early. Your project never carries another project's debt.

Four steps

  1. Submit the data package (form INS-201: title, maps, budgets, fee schedules).
  2. Independent appraisal. An MAI appraiser confirms your lots are worth at least four times the financing.
  3. Administrative annexation. Your project joins the jurisdiction's existing district by your signature as landowner; no election, no hearing.
  4. Funding. Note proceeds pay the eligible fees. You build.
Eligibility snapshot. Residential subdivisions of 2 to 50 lots in participating California jurisdictions; finished or near-finished lots; appraised value of at least four times the financing; minimum financing size set at feasibility screening. School fees are excluded at launch.

What it costs: per-project execution is targeted at $5,000 to $30,000 (founder estimate, pending vendor quotes) plus program fees, against tying up cash or double-digit interest.
